Total was nominally +1,779 better live, but that headline is meaningless because it masks wildly
different composition and enormous within-round variance. The strategy scored 24,505 on a round
where top teams almost certainly scored 100k+. The real problem isn't the total -- it's what
generated it and what variance you absorbed to get there.

### Failure 1 -- HGP: z-score mean-reversion amplified variance without adding edge

Backtest +14,896 -> Live +6,837 (-8,058). Live peaked at +20,781 then fell to -6,625 before
recovering.

The tick-by-tick volatility on HGP was almost identical in live vs backtest (avg |delta_mid|
1.68 vs 1.76). Day 3 was not a more volatile market. The variance came entirely from the
strategy itself.

The HGP engine targets positions of 135-188 units out of a 200-unit limit whenever z500 <= -1.9.
That is, it bets essentially the whole account on a z-score signal computed against a 500-tick
rolling mean. The rolling mean is NOT the fixed FV=10,000 -- it's the recent price history.
In the backtest (day 2), price started near 10,011, fell steadily to ~9,927 and stayed low, so
the z-score signal fired when the price was genuinely far below FV. In the live (day 3), price
started at 10,008 and oscillated without a clean directional move, which meant:

  - ts=0->50k:    price rose to 10,042, rolling mean caught up slowly. Strategy may have been
                  flat/short as price rose.
  - ts=50k->150k: price fell from 10,042 to 10,003, z-score relative to elevated rolling mean
                  went strongly negative. Fired a massive long signal. But price was still 3
                  ticks above FV=10,000. Strategy built a large long into an above-FV price.
                  PnL = -5,643 by ts=150k.
  - ts=150k->400k: price rose from 10,003 to 10,019-10,048. Pre-existing long paid off.
                  Peak PnL +20,781.
  - ts=400k->750k: price oscillated around 9,963-10,048, z-score fired short signals as price
                  moved back down. New short positions into a price near FV. PnL gave back to
                  +8,000 range.
  - ts=750k->999k: price fell to 9,957 then recovered to 10,001. More oscillation. Final +6,837.

The signature is a strategy that enters positions aggressively based on deviation from a
short-term floating mean, not from a stable anchor. When the floating mean itself is elevated
from a prior high, a return toward FV looks like a z-score extreme and fires a buy signal at
exactly the wrong level. The flip guards (SHORT_FLIP_GUARD_STEPS=32, LONG_FLIP_GUARD_STEPS=18)
slowed exits but could not prevent the fundamental mismatch.

What was left on the table: A simple passive MM at FV=10,000 with EDGE=2 and a modest inventory
cap of +/-80 would have avoided the -5,643 early drawdown and the peak-to-trough of 27k. Given
HGP spread mean=15.7, with a cap of 80 and reasonable fill rates, that strategy would probably
have delivered 5,000-8,000 with far less variance. The complex Noah engine added volatility
without adding net edge OOS.

### Failure 2 -- VEV_5300/5400: Anchor mispricing caused systematic adverse selection (the real
###               smoking gun)

VEV_5300: +626 -> -1,256 (-1,882). VEV_5400: +144 -> -704 (-848).

This is the most mechanically clear failure and is entirely fixable. The fair-value formula was:

    fair = 0.72 * ANCHOR[product] + 0.28 * ema

Anchors were calibrated at VEV~5,250. In the live, VEV started at 5,295.5 then fell to 5,201.5
by ts=500k before recovering to 5,232 at close. Feeding live VEV into actual Black-Scholes at
each point reveals exactly how wrong the fair values were:

  Timestamp  VEV mid   BS fair 5300  Strat fair  Error    BS fair 5400  Strat fair  Error
  ---------  --------  ------------  ----------  -------  ------------  ----------  -------
  ts=0       5,295.5   52.2          50.6        -1.6     17.8          16.5        -1.3
  ts=250k    5,237.5   27.3          43.6        +16.4    7.2           13.5        +6.3
  ts=500k    5,201.5   16.3          40.6        +24.3    3.5           12.5        +9.0
  ts=750k    5,234.5   23.8          42.7        +18.8    5.6           13.1        +7.5
  ts=999k    5,232.0   21.8          42.1        +20.3    4.8           12.9        +8.1

By the time VEV had fallen 94 ticks from open (5,295->5,201), the 72% anchor weight was pulling
strat_fair[VEV_5300] to 40 when true BS fair was 16. With taker=True and edge=0.65, the buy
trigger fires when:

    ask <= strat_fair - edge - spread ~= 37

Bots quoting near BS fair=16 posted asks at ~17. We swept those aggressively, buying at 17 while
our internal model thought fair was 40 but the true fair was 16.

The result: we built up longs in VEV_5300 at ~17-25 during the trough, then held them against a
strat_fair that kept signaling "still cheap" even as the option decayed from theta and stayed OTM
as VEV only partially recovered to 5,232. The trajectory confirms this: PnL was -2,149 at
ts=500k (bottom of VEV dip), then partly recovered to -1,256 final as VEV bounced.

The fix is one line per strike: replace the anchor formula with a live BS call:

    T_live = TTE_remaining / 365   # must track this in cache
    fair = bs_call(float(cache.get('ve_mid', ANCHOR[self.VE])), K, T_live, 0.22)

With live BS, strat_fair for VEV_5300 at VEV=5,201 would be 16, not 40. The aggressive buy
trigger would not fire at 17 (17 is not below 16 - edge). No position would build, no loss.

### Failure 3 -- VEV_5000/5100 "outperformance" was an accidental directional VEV bet, not
###               option MM alpha

VEV_5000: +1,985 -> +9,379 (+7,394). VEV_5100: +2,223 -> +6,048 (+3,826).

The same anchor distortion that hurt VEV_5300/5400 accidentally helped VEV_5000/5100 -- but
through luck, not edge. The live VEV path started at 5,295 then fell sharply, making these
deep-ITM options cheaper relative to our elevated strat_fair. At ts=500k:

  - strat_fair[VEV_5000] = 248.7 vs BS_fair = 204.3 -> 44 tick overpricing
  - Buy trigger: ask <= 248 - 1.25 - 2 = 244.75; bot ask ~= 205 -> massive taker buy sweep

We swept VEV_5000 at ~205 when true BS fair was ~204 (barely above). We built large longs near
the VEV bottom. As VEV recovered from 5,201 to 5,232, BS_fair rose from 204 to 233. Long ~275
units x +29 ticks = +7,975. That's the +7,394 delta explained.

This was the accidental opposite side of the VEV_5300 problem. The same broken anchor caused:

  - VEV_5000/5100: aggressive buying at the VEV dip -> VEV recovered -> large gain
  - VEV_5300/5400: aggressive buying at the VEV dip -> these options decayed OTM -> large loss

If VEV had not recovered (or had fallen further), VEV_5000 would have also lost. The gain was
entirely dependent on VEV recovering, which the strategy had no opinion on. The huge intraday
variance confirms this -- VEV_5000 spent time at -3,179 during the round before ending at
+9,379. This is not what an option MM strategy should look like.

### Failure 4 -- The core architecture never computed live BS fair values for options

Every voucher failure (5300/5400 losses, 5000/5100 accidental gains, 5500 inactivity) traces
back to one root cause: the fair value engine used:

    0.72 * hardcoded_anchor + 0.28 * EMA

instead of:

    bs_call(ve_mid, K, T_live, sigma)

The code already tracks cache['ve_mid'] at every tick. The Black-Scholes formula is in the
codebase. The two pieces were never connected. With live BS:

  - VEV_5000: fair would track the actual option value as VEV moves. Buys only when genuinely
              mispriced.
  - VEV_5300/5400: fair would correctly fall as VEV fell. No aggressive buying into declining
                   options.
  - TTE decay: as the round progresses (TTE: 5 -> 4), theta would be properly subtracted.
               No overcounting time value near expiry.

The linear regression proxies in REG (e.g. VEV_5300: (-1704.69, 0.334, 3.41)) are
approximations that are accurate only near the calibration VEV level. A 90-tick VEV move pushes
every strike outside the regime where the linear approximation holds.

### Failure 5 -- VEV_6000/6500 hard-disabled = certain carry missed

Both strikes explicitly return early in _trade_voucher:

    if product in ("VEV_6000", "VEV_6500"):
        return

Live VEV peaked at 5,300 -- 700 ticks below K=6,000. These were never ITM. BS fair at every
live timestamp was effectively 0. Selling 300 each at ask=1 would have collected 600 PnL with
zero risk at these vol/TTE parameters. This was identified correctly in CLAUDE.md but never
implemented. The disabling was likely a backtest artifact (some earlier test showed them as
problematic) that was not cleaned up for the final submission.

### Failure 6 -- No structural vol arb positioning despite knowing IV < RV

EDA confirmed: IV = 22%, RV = 34.2%. The market systematically underprices options vs realized
volatility. The correct structural trade is net long gamma/vega: buy ATM straddles (or strangles
on 5000/5200), delta-hedge via VEV, collect the gamma as VEV moves. This would have monetized
the 12% IV/RV gap without needing directional views.

The actual strategy was symmetric passive MM -- buying and selling options, trying to capture
spread. This leaves the vol carry entirely on the table. In a round where VEV moved 108 ticks
(3x the backtest range), a net long gamma position would have been extremely profitable
independent of direction. Instead we captured small spread PnL while the real edge sat unused.

The delta hedge also reinforced this problem. With target_ve = -net_delta * 0.5 (50% hedge
only), and a threshold of 12 + max size of 6 per step, the strategy was barely hedging. Net
voucher delta of ~256 VEV-equivalents was 50% exposed, but even the 50% hedge execution was so
slow (6 units/step) that it tracked delta with significant lag.

### Failure 7 -- VEV_5400/5500: sell-side disabled when flat, leaving free inventory

    if product in ("VEV_5400", "VEV_5500") and pos <= 0:
        sell_size = 0

When position is flat (0), selling is suppressed for VEV_5400 and VEV_5500. The rationale was a
backtest EDA observation that bot fills were only on the buy side for these strikes. OOS this
meant when these options were above fair (e.g., early in live when VEV=5,295 pushed VEV_5400
above anchor=16 to true BS fair=17.8), we couldn't initiate shorts to capture richness. We could
only buy and hold.

### What the top teams probably did differently

Based on the failure modes:

  1. Live BS pricing on all vouchers keyed off current VEV mid, updated every tick, not anchored
     to historical levels.
  2. TTE tracking in traderData -- decrement by 1 step each iteration so theta correctly prices
     into fair values as the round progresses.
  3. VEV_6000/6500 carry -- sell full limit immediately, no complex logic needed.
  4. Simpler HGP -- likely just a tight passive MM at FV=10,000 with a low position cap, no
     z-score momentum overlay.
  5. Long vega bias on ATM options -- possibly buying straddles delta-hedged, knowing IV < RV
     from the data.

The combined live PnL of 24,505 is not catastrophically wrong -- the products themselves have
limited liquidity. But the composition and path were wrong. HGP should have been a steady
8,000-10,000 with low variance; instead it was 6,837 off a +/-27k swing. VEV_5300/5400 should
have been flat-to-slightly-positive; instead -1,960 combined. VEV_6000/6500 should have been
+600 for free. The structural vol arb was worth at least another 5,000-10,000 with a proper
long-gamma posture.
